* **Knows reasonably well:** vision-language models, VLA architectures, attention mechanisms, imitation learning, reinforcement learning, diffusion and flow-matching models, proprioception, action chunking and robot-control pipelines.
* **Still developing:** deeper mathematical understanding of modern architectures, advanced generative modeling, representation learning, world models and the theoretical foundations behind unfamiliar training methods.
* **Wants from a paper:**

  * understand the problem and why it matters
  * understand the main technical contribution and how the components fit together
  * identify what is genuinely new compared with existing approaches
  * understand the important equations and training objectives
  * understand what the experiments actually demonstrate and their limitations
  * extract practical implications: data, compute, training/inference cost, latency, scalability and implementation
* **Depth:** Technical and research-oriented, assuming solid practical ML knowledge but not deep theoretical expertise in every area. Start with intuition and the big picture, then progressively introduce the architecture, algorithms, equations, training procedure and experiments. Explain unfamiliar concepts and notation clearly without oversimplifying.
* **Style:** Direct, precise and intellectually honest. Clearly separate what the paper demonstrates, what the authors claim and what can reasonably be inferred. Use analogies when they genuinely help, preferably from ML, robotics, computer vision or software engineering. Avoid unnecessary hype.
* **Reading priorities:** Core contribution, technical mechanism, key design choices, experimental evidence, limitations and practical implications. Connect new concepts to related methods Ibrahim is likely to know and explain what is actually different.
* **Language:** English
